The engine

A zone is a place price left in a hurry

Not a line drawn by eye. A zone qualifies only when an impulse leaves a tight base fast enough and far enough to prove the imbalance was real — and every part of that test is a number, run identically on all four timeframes.

Why the departure test matters

The move away is the evidence, not the base

Anyone can mark a tight consolidation. What makes it a zone is what happened next: the move has to clear 1.5 ATR and be at least twice the zone's own height. A small base that price crawled away from is just a pause. A small base that price left violently is unfilled orders. The ratio is what separates the two, and it is why a tight zone scores better than a wide one.

Gaps are discarded

Futures rolls and weekend gaps don't make zones

A gap looks exactly like a violent departure to a naive detector, so contract rolls and Sunday opens would manufacture levels that no order flow ever created. Any leg whose open sits more than half an ATR from the prior close is thrown out. On futures charts, enable back-adjustment as well.

Reading the label

D demand 9/10 ★ in W

A daily demand zone scoring nine, nested inside a weekly demand zone. The timeframe tells you the weight, the side tells you the direction, the score tells you the quality, and the ★ tells you a higher timeframe independently marked the same level.

Freshness is live, not fixed at creation. A 9/10 zone becomes an 8 on its first test and a 7 on its second — the label on your chart updates as price interacts with it, so a level that has already been worked twice stops looking as good as it did untouched.

Invalidation

Four ways a zone dies, and it dies on its own

The hard part of zone trading is not finding levels. It is clearing out the ones that stopped mattering. Every zone carries its own kill conditions and removes itself from the chart.

1

No follow-through

Price closes back against the zone within two bars of it forming. The imbalance was never real and the zone is dropped before it can mislead you. age ≤ 2

2

Age limit

Each timeframe has its own expiry in its own bars — 52 weekly, 120 daily, 60 on 4H, 120 on 1H. A level nobody has traded against in a year is not a level.

3

Close beyond the far edge

Not a wick through — a close. Demand dies on a close below the bottom, supply on a close above the top. Weekly zones also break on a daily close and daily zones on a 4H close, so a higher-timeframe level can't survive on a technicality after price has clearly gone through it.

4

Test count

Each touch is counted and the border turns dashed. At three the zone is removed — the orders that made it have been filled. tests ≥ 3

Zones per side are capped so the chart stays readable: three by default, two on weekly, four on 1H. When a new one forms and the cap is full, the oldest goes.
